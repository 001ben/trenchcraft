## September 6: physical clods in the bucket

Second review (clods still hovering; earth appearing in a different colour): three causes were found and removed. The physics ground was a bilinear patch between cell centres while the land mesh draws a fan of four triangles per cell through averaged corners, so resting clods could sit a few centimetres above or below the drawn surface on trench walls; `Simulation.surface` now evaluates that same fan. Sleeping was per clod, so a clod whose supporter had moved away or been absorbed could stay frozen in the air; every fifteen frames a flood from the ground (and from the carried load) through sleeping neighbours now marks what is supported, and anything unmarked is woken, on top of the upward wake cascade. Cut earth used to be placed inside the bowl in its own brown; following the failure-zone approach of earthmoving simulators, clods now surface out of the cut column in front of the teeth, in the land mesh's cut-soil colour, and the curling bowl sweeps them in. Such clods stay on the books until the bowl either takes them or leaves them (0.85 m from the lip or 1.5 s), so `dig()` accounting and the capacity tests are unchanged while the load shown is what is really carried. With the real ISO scoop sequence the curl sweeps in about 84% of a cut and nothing is lost through the lift and swing. All 22 tests pass; scoop, bucket, gameplay and soil browser checks pass; the loaded benchmark is unchanged at about 2.1 to 2.2 ms p95 CPU.

Follow-up after the first review (clods too large, too sticky, and some hanging in the air): each clod now stands for 0.25 litres instead of 0.6 (about 880 per full bucket, drawn near their true size with varied shapes), cohesion is a third of what it was and impacts only stick above 1.8 m/s, a tipped load leaves with uneven shoves rather than as one block, waking now cascades to every sleeping clod stacked above a woken or absorbed one (the cause of hovering clods), and joint rates ramp like hydraulics so a swing no longer jolts a heaped load off the bucket. In the lift-and-swing budget test the solver now steps about 785 carried clods in roughly 2.8 ms per frame on this desktop (worst frames about 5.5 ms); `node tools/terrain-benchmark.mjs soil --loaded` measures p95 CPU submission of 2.1 ms at 390 x 844 and 2.3 ms at 1024 x 768 with about 142,000 triangles. All 22 tests and every browser check pass.

All 22 deterministic tests pass: the fourteen gameplay, conservation, control and save tests are unchanged, the two cut-record tests are kept, and six new tests cover exact clod-to-load accounting, a full bucket riding through a lift and swing, tipping a load out onto the ground, spilling from an over-tilted bowl, version 3 save round trips with version 2 migration, and a frame-time budget with a full bucket. With the original 0.6 litre clods the lift-and-swing budget test stepped about 360 carried clods in roughly 1 ms per frame on this desktop.

Fixes found while making the physics honest: settling damping is applied only to clods in contact (otherwise free fall stalled at 0.3 m/s); a plate-crossing guard keeps clods from being pushed through the thin shell; the bowl fills from a precomputed lattice so bites placed by repeated `dig()` calls do not overlap; overlaps involving freshly placed clods are resolved without imparting velocity; fast clod-on-clod impacts stick instead of splashing; and settled clods deposit into the lowest neighbouring cell so piles spread at the grid instead of growing spikes.

`node tools/soil-check.mjs` drives lower, curl and crowd, lift with curl, swing and open with the real ISO controls, looking into the bowl from the cab side. It checks that carried clod volumes sum exactly to the load, that at least 85% of the load survives the lift and swing, that tipping releases earth, that under 0.002 m³ remains after emptying, that fewer than six clods are left loose after four seconds and that a pile exists on the ground, with no page errors and draw calls under 240. Screenshots: `.local/soil-scoop.png`, `soil-lift.png`, `soil-swing.png`, `soil-dump.png`, `soil-settled.png`.

The existing checks pass with the physics underneath: `node tools/scoop-check.mjs` (visible bed volume equals the load every frame, contact strip while cutting, no collection particles, peak 116 draw calls, about 2.2 ms p95), `node tools/bucket-check.mjs` (now asserting that an inverted bowl releases its clods while they are still in the air), `node tools/model-check.mjs` (tooth error unchanged at 9e-8 m) and `node tools/browser-check.mjs` (keyboard scoop, raised-bucket unload, mound, cab view, save/reload, pattern switch, simultaneous touch, layouts, reset). `node tools/terrain-benchmark.mjs soil --loaded` on the same desktop Edge host: p95 CPU submission 1.6 ms at 390 x 844 and 2.0 ms at 1024 x 768, draw calls unchanged at 122 and 185, triangles about 101,000 because the carried and airborne clods are instanced lumps. These are desktop measurements; phone GPU, CPU and thermal behaviour with a full bucket remain to be measured on hardware.
